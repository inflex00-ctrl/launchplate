Getting here

Tram, bus, bike — and the truth about parking

Stoneybatter is a fifteen-minute walk from the top of Grafton Street and a great deal easier to reach without a car. Here is everything we would tell you on the phone.

Luas

Museum stop on the red line, then four minutes on foot: cross Benburb Street, up Blackhall Place and right onto Marlowe Street. Trams run every six to ten minutes until roughly half eleven, which comfortably covers a 21:45 last order.

Bus

The 37, 39 and 70 all stop on Manor Street, two minutes away — get off at the church and walk down past the butcher. From the city centre it is about twelve minutes off-peak. The 46a on the quays leaves you a ten-minute walk along Blackhall Place.

Cycling

There is a Dublinbikes stand on Prussia Street, three minutes away, and it is usually the emptiest one in the district at eight o'clock. We also put two Sheffield racks outside the door — lock up under the light, it is covered by the camera over the porch.

Parking

We have no car park — the yard is where the wood is stacked. On-street parking on Marlowe Street and Arbour Hill is paid until 19:00 and free after, so an eight o'clock table usually parks for nothing. If you are here for lunch, the Smithfield multi-storey is a seven-minute walk and takes cards at the barrier.

Accessibility

Step-free from the Marlowe Street footpath straight into the dining room, with an accessible WC on the ground floor and space to turn between the tables. The Loft, though, is up one flight of stairs and there is no lift — a 130-year-old coach house will only give us so much. Large-print menus and the full allergen folder are at the host stand, and assistance dogs are always welcome. Tell us when you book and we will hold a table near the door.
