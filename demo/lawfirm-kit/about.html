A firm built to outlast its founders

Halloway & Finch has practised from the same street in Boston’s Financial District since 1968. Three offices, thirty-four lawyers, and one rule that has not changed: tell the client the truth first.

Our story

Fifty-seven years of the same conversation

Arthur Halloway and Clement Finch were not trying to build an institution. They were two lawyers who had grown tired of being told which clients they could take, and who believed that a firm ought to be small enough for every partner to know every matter.

What began as a waterfront practice for shipping agents and printers became, over five decades, the firm that New England’s family businesses call when something has gone wrong — or, better, when they can see that it might. Our clients now include manufacturers in their fourth generation, hospital systems, developers, and the founders of companies that did not exist a decade ago.

The work has changed completely. The way we take it on has not. A partner still meets every new client personally, still writes an honest assessment before any money is spent, and still says no to matters this firm is not the right one to handle.

Meet the attorneys

What we stand for

Four commitments, in writing

These are printed in every engagement letter the firm sends. If we fall short of one of them, we would rather hear it from you than from a review.

01

Candour before comfort

We would rather lose a matter than take one we do not believe in. Clients are told what we actually think, in the first meeting, even when it costs us the instruction.

02

The file belongs to the client

Everything we write, you can read. No internal memorandum says something different from what we have told you, and every client leaves with a complete copy of their file.

03

Small teams, senior hands

A partner is responsible for every matter and does the work that matters. We have never staffed a case to fill a budget, and we never will.

04

A price you agreed to

Fixed fees for defined work, written estimates for the rest, and a conversation before any step that changes the number. Surprise is a failure of service, not a fact of practice.

History

How the firm grew

1968

Two lawyers and one filing cabinet

Arthur Halloway and Clement Finch open a two-room practice on Ashcroft Street, representing the shipping agents, printers and family shops of Boston’s waterfront. The partnership agreement they signed runs to a page and a half; its opening clause about candour with clients is still in ours.

1981

The first business litigation group

As New England’s manufacturers consolidated, the firm followed its clients into the courtroom. The litigation practice that now employs eleven lawyers began with a single shareholder dispute the founders were told not to take.

1994

A generation arrives

Eleanor Halloway joins as an associate, three years before the firm elects its first woman partner. By the end of the decade the practice has doubled and moved into the Suite 1900 offices it still occupies.

2003

Providence

A Rhode Island office opens to serve development and permitting clients along the Narragansett corridor, and the firm is admitted to practise in a second state.

2016

New leadership, same terms

Eleanor Halloway is elected managing partner. The firm publishes its fee policy — fixed prices for defined work, written estimates for everything else — and has not billed outside it since.

2021

Hartford, and a third state

The Connecticut office opens with four attorneys, completing the tri-state footprint the founders sketched on the back of a docket sheet in 1974.

Community

Work we are not paid for

Every attorney at this firm carries at least one uncompensated matter a year. It is written into how we evaluate lawyers for partnership, which is the only way a commitment like this survives a busy quarter.
