The carte

What we're cooking this week

The menu is rewritten every Tuesday, an hour after the Cloughjordan van pulls away and we can see what we actually have. Some of it will still be here on Saturday. Some of it goes by Wednesday and does not come back until next year.

The full list

Prices include VAT and there is no service charge — what you see is what lands on the bill. If something has just gone, we will tell you before you get attached to it.

Starters

Order two or three between you and keep going.

Hearth-charred octopus GF N €19

Slow-braised for four hours, then finished directly on the embers until the edges go crisp. Romesco made with Wicklow hazelnuts, burnt lemon, a lot of good oil.

Oak-smoked beetroot VE GF €16

Cloughjordan beetroot buried in the ash overnight, dressed like a tartare with capers, shallot and a horseradish cream that will wake you up.

Broderick's oysters, four GF €16

Carlingford rocks, opened to order. Two raw with a cider mignonette, two laid straight on the coals for ninety seconds with beech-smoked butter. Six for €23 if you are serious.

Hearth bread & smoked butter V €12

Ballybrado flour, milled in Cahir, baked at six in the morning in the falling heat of the fire. Cultured butter smoked over beech and whipped with sea salt. Refills are free; ask.

Goatsbridge trout, cured & lightly smoked GF €17

Kilkenny trout cured a full day in salt and juniper, then hung twenty minutes over cooling oak — enough to perfume it, not enough to cook it. Buttermilk, cucumber, dill oil.

Velvet Cloud curd & ember leeks V GF €15

Mayo sheep's milk curd under leeks charred whole in the ash and peeled back to the sweet middle. Toasted seeds, a spoon of green oil, black pepper.

Grilled mussels, 'nduja butter HOT €14

Kish Fish mussels opened on the grill bars, each one spooned with a hot, red, faintly reckless butter. Bread comes with it because you will want it.

Mains

All of it over Irish oak and beech. There is no gas in the building.

Ember-roasted lamb GF €34

Shoulder from Ballyknocken in Co. Wicklow, cooked eight hours beside the fire until it gives way to a spoon. Wild garlic when it's in, salsa verde when it's not.

Whole turbot over the coals, for two GF €38

Whatever the Kish Fish day boats land on Thursday — turbot most weeks, brill when it isn't — cooked whole in the fish clamp and finished with a hot vinaigrette of its own juices. Priced per person, minimum two, about twenty-five minutes.

Dry-aged sirloin over oak GF €36

Forty days on the bone from a single Meath herd, grilled hard and rested on the warm stone beside the hearth. Bone-marrow butter, watercress, a wedge of charred lemon. Served pink unless told otherwise.

Ember-grilled duck, blackcurrant GF €32

Crown hung a week, skin rendered slowly over low embers, sauce made from the bones and last summer's blackcurrants. Sharp enough to keep the fat honest.

Hake in the fish clamp GF €29

A thick cut of Kish Fish hake grilled skin-down over white ash, with mussels, sea beet and a broth built on the trimmings. Clean, hot and gone in ten minutes.

Pici, Cloughjordan tomatoes & smoked ricotta V €26

Ballybrado flour rolled by hand every afternoon — badly at first, then well. Tomatoes roasted in the mouth of the fire until they collapse, basil, ricotta smoked in-house.

Fire-roasted celeriac VE GF N €24

A whole celeriac from Cloughjordan, salt-crusted and buried in embers for three hours, then carved at the pass like a small joint. Hazelnut picada, charred apple, its own roasting juices.

Sides

Two between two is plenty. Three is a decision you make together.

Hearth potatoes in beef fat GF €7

Boiled, crushed, then dropped into the dripping tray under the grill until the edges shatter.

Charred hispi cabbage, Velvet Cloud V GF €8

Halved, laid cut-side down until properly black, spooned with Mayo sheep's yoghurt and toasted caraway.

Cloughjordan leaves, mustard dressing VE GF €6

Whatever is standing in the market garden this week, dressed sharp. A palate cleanser wearing a side dish's clothes.

Ember onions, thyme honey V GF €7

Whole onions cooked in their skins in the ash until jammy, split and glazed with honey from the Cabra allotments.

Fire-roasted carrots, hazelnut dukkah VE GF N €8

Roasted whole with their tops on, dressed in dukkah we grind on Tuesdays and a squeeze of blood orange.

Grilled greens, anchovy & lemon GF €9

Whatever is bitter and in season — cavolo, chard, sprouting broccoli — hit hard by the flame, then anchovy butter.

Desserts

Tomás Delgado's section. He is Galician and he does not rush.

Burnt honey tart V €11

Brown butter pastry, honey from the hives on the Cabra allotments, caramelised until it is very nearly too far. Crème fraîche to cut it. It has not left the menu since 2019 and there would be letters if it did.

Oak-smoked chocolate, sea salt V GF €12

Cream infused with cooled oak embers before it ever meets the chocolate. Dense, savoury, faintly of bonfire — the one dessert that argues with the wine.

Tarta de Santiago V GF N €10

Tomás's grandmother's recipe — almond, lemon zest, the cross of Saint James dusted on top. He will tell you it is better in Galicia. He is probably right.

Roast pear, buttermilk & honeycomb V GF €10

Pears roasted in the dying heat overnight so they go amber the whole way through, buttermilk ice cream, a shard of honeycomb.

Velvet Cloud ice cream, Ballybrado crumb V €9

Sheep's milk ice cream churned each morning, toasted brown-flour crumb, a spoon of last summer's blackcurrants. The quiet one.

By the glass

Fourteen open at any time; these six are pouring this week.

Domaine Landron ‘Amphibolite’ · Muscadet, Loire €11

Chalky, saline, faintly of the sea. The glass Aoife pours when someone announces they don't like white wine.

Sepp Moser Grüner Veltliner · Kremstal, Austria €10

Biodynamic, white pepper and green apple. Cuts through anything that has spent time in smoke.

Kumpf et Meyer ‘Grande Ourse’ · Alsace €13

Six days on skins. Apricot skin, bitter orange and a grippy finish that stands up to anything off the coals.

Cantina Giardino ‘Sophia’ Fiano · Campania €14

Amphora-aged, waxy and wild. Our most-argued-about glass, which is exactly why we keep it open.

Domaine de la Sénéchalière Gamay · Loire €12

Served cool from the cellar. Crushed raspberry and wet stone — the red we drink with fish and refuse to apologise for.

Bodegas Gratias Bobal Rosado · Manchuela, Spain €9

Dry, high-altitude rosado from old bush vines. Starts the evening better than almost anything else on the list.

Cocktails & aperitivo

Five only, all built at the bar, none of them blue.

Oak old fashioned €15

Irish single pot still, demerara, and a glass smoked with a splint from the same log store that feeds the hearth. Stirred, never hurried.

Burnt orange negroni €14

Oranges halved and blackened on the grill, then steeped in the campari for a fortnight. Bitter, smoky, dangerous at 5.30pm.

Smoked rosemary gimlet €13

Gin, lime cordial made in-house on Mondays, a rosemary sprig lit and put out under the glass.

Sloe & sherry highball €13

Manzanilla, sloe gin from hedgerows near Ballyknocken, soda and a long strip of lemon. Salty and low-proof.

House amaro spritz €12

Our own amaro — gentian, birch, spent coffee — lengthened with a dry pét-nat. The drink to have while you wait for the table.

Soft, coffee & tea

Nobody at this table has to be drinking wine.

Zero-proof aperitivo €6

Bitter, orange-peel and gentian, built like the negroni and just as slow to drink.

Cabra honey lemonade €5.50

Made each morning with allotment honey and far more lemon than is sensible.

Hedgerow cordial & soda €5

Elderflower in early summer, blackberry and apple by October. Whatever Ruth picked on her day off.

Loose-leaf tea €4.50

Assam, jasmine green, or a lemon verbena tisane from the courtyard pots. In a pot, not a bag.

Espresso, macchiato, flat white €4

A single Irish-roasted lot, changed every few weeks. We will happily make it decaf and we will not raise an eyebrow.

Filter coffee €3.50

Brewed by the jug after eight o'clock and poured freely until we put the chairs up.

Before you order The full allergen list is on the wall by the door and every server carries a copy — tell us at the table and it takes one sentence, not a negotiation. We keep a separate coeliac-safe prep area and a set of boards and pans that never meet flour. The vegan tasting menu needs 48 hours' notice, because Maren wants to buy for it properly rather than improvise on the night. If a dish here can be changed for you, it usually can be; if it genuinely can't, we will say so.

Questions we get at the pass

Properly, once a week. The Cloughjordan van arrives Tuesday morning and the board gets rewritten by lunchtime, so the list you are reading on a Monday is last week's. Four or five dishes — the octopus, the lamb, the beetroot, the honey tart — have earned tenure and rarely move. Everything else is fair game, and a dish can vanish mid-service if the fish runs out.

Tuesdays and Wednesdays only, €25 corkage per bottle, two bottles maximum per table. We would rather you brought something you have been saving than something we already have on the list — and if it is genuinely special, pour Aoife a taste and the corkage tends to be forgotten.

Children are very welcome at the weekend lunch sittings and at the Sunday long lunch, less so at half nine on a Saturday night. There is no separate children's menu — instead the kitchen will do a smaller plate of the pasta, the potatoes or a piece of plain grilled fish for around €12. Two high chairs, and the changing table is in the accessible WC on the ground floor.
