Brand identity · Packaging · Art direction

Halden Optics

A fifty-seven-year-old Norwegian instrument maker, invisible to the very people who most wanted what it made. We gave it a way to be looked at — and looked through.

Client

Halden Optics AS
Tromsø, Norway

Scope

Positioning, naming architecture, identity, packaging, photographic direction, guidelines

Duration

14 weeks design
11 months rollout

Team

Four from Meridian, plus a photographer and a packaging engineer

01

The challenge

Halden has been grinding glass in Tromsø since 1968. Their objectives sit inside instruments used by glaciologists, ornithologists and surveyors in genuinely unpleasant places, and they have a reputation among people who know, which is a small and shrinking group.

Ninety per cent of revenue came from institutional tenders won on specification sheets. But the buyers were changing. A new generation of independent field researchers — grant-funded, self-equipping, buying online at eleven at night — had never heard of them. Online, Halden looked like a component supplier: a 1994 logotype, four sub-brands with no visible relationship to one another, and product photography borrowed from a reseller catalogue.

Meanwhile a wave of well-funded consumer optics brands were outspending them on story while under-delivering on glass. Halden was losing a market it deserved to own, to companies making worse instruments.

The brief we were handed said “make the website better”. The brief we wrote back said: give a fifty-seven-year-old engineering company a way to describe what it is good at, in language a twenty-nine-year-old glaciologist recognises as true.

02

The approach

We started by going north. Two of us spent nine days in Tromsø — four in the grinding shop, five with customers in the field, including two days on a glacier holding a tripod and asking questions between measurements.

What came back was a company that talked exclusively in millimetres and grams, and customers who talked exclusively about what they had been able to see. That gap became the whole strategy.

Made to be looked through. The instrument is not the point. It is the least interesting object in the story. What matters is the moment it disappears and the thing on the other side of it becomes clear. Every decision after that sentence had somewhere to be measured against.

  • A mark drawn from the object itself Two arcs taken from the true curvature of the FS‑8 objective lens, in cross-section. It is not a metaphor for precision; it is a measurement.
  • Four sub-brands collapsed into three tiers Field, Survey and Reference — named for the work, not the engineering. A distributor can now explain the whole range in one sentence.
  • Colour from the anodising bath The palette is sampled from Halden’s own finishing process: a deep instrument blue, an oxidised ember, and the bone of a printed spec card.
  • Photography that admits the weather No products floating on white. Instruments in gloved hands, fogged, scratched, in use. We wrote the brief and shot the first two campaigns with the studio’s photographer.
  • Packaging that becomes the case Designed with a packaging engineer so the box that ships the instrument is the box that lives in the rucksack. Nothing is thrown away.
03

The outcome

The identity rolled out over eleven months across fourteen markets, starting with the 2025 range and the direct-to-consumer store, then distributor materials, trade stands and the technical documentation library.

The number Halden cares about is direct-to-consumer revenue, which had been a rounding error. Twelve months after launch it had more than tripled, and for the first time the company could see who its customers actually were.

The number we care about is quieter: distributor onboarding fell from six weeks to nine days, because the range finally explains itself. A brand system earns its keep when other people can operate it without you.

Results

214% Growth in direct-to-consumer revenue, first twelve months
4→1 Sub-brands consolidated into a single three-tier range
−38% Packaging cost per unit, after the case-as-box redesign
9 Days to onboard a new distributor, down from six weeks
