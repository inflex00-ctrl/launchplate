The basics

What the tool is called and what it does. Write the tagline the way you would say it to another engineer, not the way you would put it on a billboard.

Exactly as you write it yourself. Up to 40 characters.

The page a developer should land on, including https://.

For the review decision only. Never published, never sold.

One sentence, 20–90 characters. “Git-push deploys for Go, Rust and Node with no YAML to write” is the right shape.

A paragraph or two. What problem it solves, who it is for, and what it deliberately does not do. Our editor will rewrite this after testing, so plain and honest beats polished.

Nothing is saved until you reach the end.

Category & tags

One category only. If two look right, pick the one where a developer would go looking for you — the tags below cover the rest.

Where does it belong? *

Up to five, comma separated, lower case with dashes: open-source, postgres, self-hosted. Where a tag already exists in the directory we will reuse it rather than create a near-duplicate.

Placement

Paid placements move a listing up the page. They do not change a word of the review, and every one of them carries a label. If in doubt, stay on Free — you can upgrade later without resubmitting.

Choose a placement

Paying changes where a listing sits, never what it says. A sponsored tool that does not survive testing is refunded rather than published — see the placement questions for the whole policy.
