The studio

Twelve people
who still draw.

Meridian has been independent since 2014. No holding company, no investors, no growth target — just a deliberate size that lets the people you meet be the people who do the work.

How it started, and why it stayed small.

In 2014 Johanna Lind left a large studio in Berlin because she had spent eleven months on a project without drawing anything. Tomas Ferreira left a newsroom the same summer for a related reason: he was writing about companies he was not allowed to help fix. They took a room above a tile shop in Lisbon, and the first client was a bakery that could not explain why its bread cost three euros.

The bakery is still trading. So is the studio, now with twelve people across two cities and a policy that has not changed since the second year: we take nine projects a year, and the founders work on all of them.

We have been asked several times to become something bigger — a merger in 2019, an acquisition in 2022. Both would have made everyone involved more money and made the work worse, because the thing clients are actually buying is a small number of experienced people paying close attention. That does not scale, and we stopped pretending it might.

What we are good at is the moment before the design: the argument, the sentence, the decision about what a company is actually for. Everything visual follows from that, and it is why our proposals read more like editing than decoration.

How we operate

Four rules we
have not broken.

These are not aspirations on a wall. They are the reasons we have turned down work, and they are worth reading before you brief us.

Say the hard thing early

The uncomfortable observation on week two is worth more than a beautiful deck on week ten. We would rather lose a project honestly than win it by agreeing with you.

Make it, don’t present it

We show work in the medium it will live in — a real page, a real pack, a real screen — because a logo on a grey square has never told anyone anything true.

Leave a system, not a dependency

The measure of a good handover is how rarely you need to call us afterwards. We build things your team can operate, and we write down why every rule exists.

Small on purpose

Twelve people, two cities, nine projects a year. Growth would mean the founders stop drawing, and the reason clients come to us is that the founders still draw.
